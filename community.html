All Saints Church, Saxby

All Saints Church is a historic Grade II listed parish church situated immediately neighbouring our village hall on Church Lane. Together, the church and hall have formed the spiritual and social focal point of Saxby All Saints since the mid-nineteenth century.

Parish Heritage

Designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott

Grade II Listed • Rebuilt 1845–1849

Christian worship in Saxby stretches back nearly a millennium, with the settlement recorded as Saxebi in the Domesday Book of 1086. Between 1845 and 1849, the earlier medieval parish church was substantially rebuilt to designs by Sir George Gilbert Scott — one of Victorian Britain’s most celebrated Gothic Revival architects, famed nationally for the Albert Memorial and London’s St Pancras Chambers. Commissioned under the patronage of the local Barton (later Hope-Barton) family of Saxby Hall during the very same years they founded our neighbouring 1846 village schoolhouse, Scott worked in an authentic late 13th-century Decorated Gothic style while carefully incorporating surviving medieval stonework into the new structure.

In 1873, John Hope Barton and parishioners enriched the church’s silhouette with the addition of its distinctive west clock tower, designed by the architect Neville. Topped with an embattled parapet, crocketed corner pinnacles, carved angle gargoyles, and a striking pyramidal roof with a wrought-iron finial, the tower caught the eye of architectural historian Sir Nikolaus Pevsner as one of North Lincolnshire’s most memorable country church landmarks.

Stained Glass Windows & Interior Craftsmanship

Stepping inside reveals richly preserved Victorian craftsmanship, from the carved ashlar stone pulpit, ornate timber altar rails, and sculpted pew ends to historic Barton family wall tablets. Most celebrated of all is the church’s stained glass: alongside the pointed three-light traceried East Window in the chancel (framed by nook shafts and carved headstops), the North Aisle East Window (1876) was painted by the renowned Victorian master Charles Eamer Kempe (C.E. Kempe), depicting the Virgin and Child in his signature luminous, delicate tones.
